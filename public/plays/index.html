Simple offensive plays

Four half-court sets for 15 to 17 year old boys. Start with the give-and-go and the UCLA cut, then add Horns once players stop drifting into each other’s spots.

Reading the diagrams

Each play shows two phases side by side. Players are numbered 1 to 5, with 1 as the point guard and 5 as the center.

What is Horns

Horns is a formation, not one specific play. The point guard has the ball at the top of the key, two players stand at the elbows (the corners of the free-throw line), and the other two sit in the corners. From above, the two elbow players look like horns sticking up from the lane.

Many actions start from that one setup:

  • Ball screen from either side. The point guard can use either big, so the defense can’t sit on one side. This is the play below.
  • Double ball screen. Both bigs screen at once and the guard picks a direction.
  • Hand-off or high-post entry. Pass to an elbow big, who hands off to a cutter or looks for a backdoor cut.
  • Big-to-big action. One elbow big screens for the other, which works well against defenses that switch.

The floor is already spaced, with two shooters in the corners and the middle of the lane open. Kids can run several looks from one alignment, and the defense has to guess which side the action is coming from.

Horns ball screen

Two bigs at the elbows. One screens for the point guard, then rolls to the rim while the other pops out.

  • 1 sets up his man by stepping away from the screen first, then comes off shoulder to shoulder with the screener.
  • First look is 5 rolling to the rim.
  • If the help defender drops in on 5, kick out to 4 at the top or 3 in the corner.

Give-and-go, five out

Everyone starts outside the arc. The best teaching play for spacing and for punishing a defender who turns his head.

  • After the pass, 1 takes a hard step away from the ball, then cuts in front of his defender with a hand up as a target.
  • If 1 isn’t open, he clears through to the far corner.
  • Everyone rotates one spot and you run it again from the other side. That rotation is the start of a simple motion offense.

UCLA cut

Pass to the wing, cut off a high-post screen to the block, then the big pops back to the top.

  • 5 has to hold the screen, not move into the cutter.
  • 1 cuts tight enough to brush 5’s shoulder.
  • If the cut isn’t there, 2 passes to 5 at the top to swing it and flow into a second action, such as a down screen for 3.

“Box” baseline out of bounds

Four players in a box around the lane. A quick look at the rim plus two safe outlets.

  • The inbounder slaps the ball to start, so everyone moves at once.
  • After screening, 4 seals his man and is often open himself.
  • 2 at the top is the “just get it in” option when the defense switches everything.
